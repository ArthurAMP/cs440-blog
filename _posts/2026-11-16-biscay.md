## Key Idea

Cellular networks have some key differences from regular LAN connectivity. For one, Cellular networks are designed for moving phones that will have variable distance from the antennas and will require constant and seamless handoffs. End to end signals, like the ones most congestion control algorithms are based on, get significantly noisier due to the differences in Cellular networks.

This paper's insight is that the phone modem actually has all the information we need to compute the cellular link's available bandwidth, as it logs the radio performance indicators consistently. As the cellular link is most often the bottleneck, you can use the computed bandwidth to set the congestion window. However, as the cellular link might not be the entire bottleneck, they keep computing the available bandwidth based on E2E signals and use the lower of the two. This leads to getting almost the same throughput with significant less queuing. 

## Critique

The explanation they give for the fact that every algorithm's results are better in mobility is because the dataset is skewed regarding 4G and 5G, as 80% of 5G data is mobile and 20% static. I buy this explanation, but this could hide some other problems. An algorithm that loses out on 5g static would be hard to spot, for example. I don't know if it would be a problem in this paper, but I thought that was interesting.
