## Key Idea

They first present a specific blind spot for Traffic Engineering analysis tools: a case where a failure happens, and you must prepare for the highest impact probable failures (with the example being a case where consecutive fiber cuts caused an outage on Microsoft's production WAN in Africa). The specific gap they explore is that prior work would optimize for the lowest performance cases after a failure, when that doesn't necessarily matter. For example, consider two cases: In one, the post-failure network suffers from congestion with 2 units of demand, while the healthy one handles 3. In the other, the post-failure congests with 10 units of demand, while the healthy one handles 100. Finding the later case is much more impactful, as in the first one the failure barely caused any material impact. 

The way they achieve that is by finding the maximum of the traffic the healthy network can handle minus the traffic the unhealthy network can handle. This ends up being essentially a game theory problem, as you're trying to create an adversarial demand by assuming the TE agent will attempt its best response against the failure. MetaOpt, the tool they used to analyze the heuristic, needs the heuristic to be a convex optimization problem. They achieve that by decoupling the failure selection and adversarial traffic input from the TE response. Thus, the failure selection is a constant for the second optimization problem, and you can use MetaOpt. And leveraging all of this, Raha is able to find risks on the network and create alerts for them. 

## Critique

I think the idea is clever and especially the recording of the presentation was really good at communicating the value of finding these high-impact potential degradations and generating alerts for them. I also was sufficiently convinced by the presentation that prior tools are limited in the tasks Raha solves by design. 

It might be mainly due to a lack of experience from my part, but Section 5, "Modeling unhealthy networks" was unreadable for me. I had to watch the presentation video for MetaOpt and I had to use LLMs to decode the equations to understand what they were saying, and I couldn't shake the feeling that it was too simple for it to have been that hard for me.

Because I spent so much time trying to understand the idea, I didn't have time to dig into many specific details. One that was immediately noticeable, however, is that on Section 5.1 the product assumes independence between failures. Obviously it would be impratical to make a generalized model that doesn't assume that, but such assumptions shouldn't go unjustified or at least unmentioned. Especially with the real-world example being consecutive fiber cuts in Africa, which plausibly could be correlated.

## Connections

Leveraging [Invariant Analysis](/cs440-blog/2026/09/30/islands-and-peninsulas/) to determine probability of a given demand would be a key addition to this work. That would create the possibility of a list of probable inputs instead of optimizing for the worst-case input, increasing the practicality of possible outages. Especially because Raha already supports this, as outlined on the "Enforce a strongly connected network..." paragraph on Section 5.1. So one could design a search over Invariant-informed constructed demands and feed it to Raha.
