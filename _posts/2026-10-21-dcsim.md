## Key Idea

By keeping a queue of much smaller packets together (refered as Sim packets from now on) with the queue of Data packets you must send, and keeping a "shadow simulated network" that mimicks the real network but runs at (Sim size / Data size) speed, you can check whether a data packet would likely arrive by first sending its corresponding Sim packet and waiting for acknowledgement. 

This idea is then leveraged by always transmitting a Sim packet and only sending a Data packet if its Sim packet was acknowledged. Then, they calculated which rate you should send the packets to fill out the link fairly, reaching a figure of C/(a+2.1) when accounting for acknowledgement overhead, with C being link speed and "a" being (Data size / Sim size). 

They evaluated this idea on an emulator, achieving higher utilization rates, lower CCT, and 0% packet loss on non-degenerate cases. Additionally, it did better than baseline even in degenerate cases.

## Critique

This idea is awesome and it was immediately convincing to me. After reading this paper more carefully, I wish I chose this for my reproduction project. 

The one part that I wish was better clarified is how a deployment of this idea would look like, as the fixed delay requirement is a real complication. Would it be a userspace application that leverages SEND_ON_TIMESTAMP or a different capability to specify the delay? I belive most NICs actually used in current AI training would support that, but it is a negative point regarding the "commodity switch" goal. Would it be implemented at the firmware level on the NICs? That would likely satisfy the requirements a little better, but it wouldn't be something an individual can implement and use. Would it be implemented in programmable cores in SmartNICs? That again goes against the commodity NIC requirement, but it would give the end user more control. I know the paper is about the theoretical concept rather than actual deployment, but I think that would be a neat addition.
