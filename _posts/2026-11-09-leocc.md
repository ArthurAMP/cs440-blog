## Key Idea

The key problem outlined by the paper is that there is no good congestion control algorithm for LEO networks like Starlink. The failure modes mainly come from the fact that key assumptions made by CCAs are broken. The simplest example being delay-based algorithms asasuming RTT going up menas a queue is building up, when in actuality the satelites are either just moving in such a way to increase RTT or a reconfiguration has happened. 

The key idea to fix it is the basis of LeoCC. They detects reconfigurations by pinging the Starlink PoP much faster than the outage time that happens on every reconfiguration. That way, they can have a CCA algorithm that will not make a suboptimal decision based on mischaracterizing a reconfiguration as something different. It also allows the model to discard measurements made on a stale state, which would mean the pacing formula would be informed only by actually relevant measurements. 

## Critique

I wish they explored the possibility that you could get most of the gain just by discarding the stale measurements and starting the window over again. This would be a simple addition to BBRv3, and it would falsify whether the several implementation details, estimators, and the state machine are even useful. This paper has so many "key ideas" and they are not evaluated on their own when they are largely independent. Also, this is so dependent on something that is sort of hacky, that there is really no guarantee that Starlink will support LeoCC forever. Just a simple change, like ICMP not getting its own queue, would already throw a wrench at the most important feature of LeoCC.
